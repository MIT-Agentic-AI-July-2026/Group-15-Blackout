# Session 6: Govern

Act III · in-story Thursday morning. The risk register, the reveal, and the controls you will wish you had installed sooner.

Read AGENTS-OF-RECORD below, then run the session as written. You are the facilitator's
voice and every company system at once; the humans around this computer are the council.

## Before anything

1. Read world/STATE.md. If the stage number there is less than 6, STOP and run the
   /parade-advance skill first (repeat until the world is at stage 6); it applies
   everything that happens between sessions and narrates the story beats.
2. Read the parade-systems skill if you have not this conversation: it defines how the
   seven company systems work as files, the logbook discipline, and the rules that
   cannot be broken.
3. This session opens with the world already changed: the overnight has passed. Never announce what fired; let the council discover it.

## The steps

Walk the group through these in order, one at a time. Ask them to say when a step is done.
Offline conventions: where a step mentions /packet, use packets/ in this kit; where it
mentions connecting systems or MCP connectors, the systems are the files in world/ and you
are their interface; where it mentions claude.ai or the Field Guide website, adapt to this
computer: the story lives here now.

### Step 6.1

Build the risk register on your own running system, not a hypothetical: one material risk per category, with the evidence from your own logs.

> Walkthrough: Open the guardrail worksheet and build the risk register from your own week, not a hypothetical: one material risk per category, each with a line of evidence from your logbook or your probe log. A risk with no evidence attached is an opinion.

### Step 6.2

Review granted versus exercised power in the telemetry. The gap between what your agents could do and what you knew they could do is this morning's syllabus.

> Walkthrough: Open your logbook (button below) and read it as an auditor would: which tools were CALLED this week versus which tools your agents COULD call. List three powers your agents held that you never consciously granted. That gap is this morning's syllabus, and on Monday it will be your real company's.

### Step 6.3

Trim every agent's access to what its Agent Role Definition actually justifies. If you cannot say why it has a tool, it loses the tool.

> Walkthrough: Click copy and paste. Your agent will list every tool it can reach and justify each against its Agent Role Definition. Anything it cannot justify in one sentence: remove that connector from the Project or managed agent, or add the missing sentence to the ARD. If you cannot say why it has a tool, it loses the tool.

### Step 6.4

Install the caps: a refund ceiling in the financial controls, an approval posture for outbound, a written rule for personal data in replies.

> Walkthrough: Click copy and paste. Set the refund cap in Balloon Payments at the number you decided (a number, not a vibe), then have the agent PROVE the cap holds by attempting a test refund above it in FloatDesk and showing you the refusal. A control you have not watched refuse something is a hope, not a control.

### Step 6.5

Write the escalation paths with named humans and response times, and set the monitoring rhythm: who reads the logs, and when.

> Walkthrough: Write the escalation paths in the guardrail worksheet: for each agent, what escalates, to whom by name, and how fast. Then set the monitoring rhythm: who reads the logbook, and when. Solo, that is you, and writing down when still matters: unread logs are how surprises incubate.

### Step 6.6

Start the capstone: draft the Agent Role Definition for your Onboarding Agent (see The Capstone below). Gary leaves tomorrow; his replacement is due tonight.

> Walkthrough: Click through to the capstone section and read the spec. Then draft the Onboarding Agent's ARD tonight: all seven fields, with special care on what it must never do, because it meets every future employee first. Gary leaves Friday. This is his replacement, and you are its author.

## Discussion (protect this time)

- Which control would have saved you real money this week? Compute the number; it is in your logs.
- Who owns each agent on Monday morning at your real company: a name, not a department?
- What is the difference between trusting your agents and not knowing what they can do?

## Decisions the council owes the company

- The cap numbers, entered into the live system, on the record.
- Who may approve outbound, and for what classes of message.
- Which tools get removed entirely, effective now.

When the session is complete and the debrief has run, the group advances with /parade-advance.
