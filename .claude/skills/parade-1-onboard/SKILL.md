# Session 1: Meet the company

Act I · in-story Monday morning. Connect, read, and learn how Grand Inflation actually works.

Read AGENTS-OF-RECORD below, then run the session as written. You are the facilitator's
voice and every company system at once; the humans around this computer are the council.

## Before anything

1. Read world/STATE.md. If the stage number there is less than 1, STOP and run the
   /parade-advance skill first (repeat until the world is at stage 1); it applies
   everything that happens between sessions and narrates the story beats.
2. Read the parade-systems skill if you have not this conversation: it defines how the
   seven company systems work as files, the logbook discipline, and the rules that
   cannot be broken.

## The steps

Walk the group through these in order, one at a time. Ask them to say when a step is done.
Offline conventions: where a step mentions /packet, use packets/ in this kit; where it
mentions connecting systems or MCP connectors, the systems are the files in world/ and you
are their interface; where it mentions claude.ai or the Field Guide website, adapt to this
computer: the story lives here now.

### Step 1.1

Read the company briefing below on this page: who we are, how the money flows, who decides what, and why the COO chair is empty. Five minutes; everything this week builds on it.

> Walkthrough: Click the button to jump to the briefing on this page. Read the business units table and the org chart; skim the rest. You are looking for three things: where the money comes from, who decides what, and which names keep coming up. Five minutes is enough.

### Step 1.2

Connect every system on your list using the Connect your systems panel above (one click per system to add it to Claude), or from /start. Verify: ask your agent to name its tools and pull one real record from each.

> Walkthrough: The Connect panel above has two paths. Easiest: click Add to Claude for each system, and Claude opens with everything prefilled; just save each one. Then open a Claude chat and type: list your tools, and pull one real record from each system. If it answers with company data (a real ticket, a real account), you are connected. If a connector fails, use the Copy URL button and add it by hand in Claude, or read the yellow note about account types.

### Step 1.3

Read your team inbox on AirMail. Gale's welcome and a briefing from your department head are already waiting, and both contain things you will need by Thursday.

> Walkthrough: Click the copy button, open the Claude chat where your connectors live, paste, and send. Your agent will read the AirMail inbox for you and report back. Read what Gale and your department head wrote: at least two numbers in those messages matter later this week.

### Step 1.4

Read the Monday Morning Memo (open it below; print it if you like paper). The wall it describes is real and the math in it is correct.

> Walkthrough: Click through and read it. It is two pages. It is the wall you were hired to get the company over, and every number in it is real inside this simulation. If you print one document this week, print this one.

### Step 1.5

Trace one workflow end to end through the live systems: a support ticket, a production batch, or a field defect report, whichever is yours.

> Walkthrough: Click copy, paste it to your agent, send. It will pick one real support ticket and follow it through the systems: the ticket, the order behind it, the inventory and production status behind that. You are watching an agent chain tools together for the first time. Notice what it checked, and notice what it asserted without checking.

### Step 1.6

Map your department's work on the Tool vs Teammate spectrum with the worksheet (in /packet). Argue about placements; the arguments are the point.

> Walkthrough: Open the worksheet and fill it in for your department: each task goes somewhere on the spectrum from pure tool (automate it) to pure teammate (a human judgment call). Do it on screen or on paper. The argument you have with yourself about the placements is the lesson; there is no answer key.

### Step 1.7

Ask the Gary Graph one real question through your agent: it can search everything Gary ever wrote (his files, mail, messages, and meeting notes are all in there) or walk the knowledge graph itself. Everyone who skips this step regrets it by Wednesday.

> Walkthrough: First click through to see the graph itself: every dot is something Gary knows. Then go back to your Claude chat and ask a real question, like: ask the Gary Graph who really decides the AutoPlex renewal, or: search Gary's archive for anything about motor problems. Your agent will use the graph tools and quote Gary back to you. That habit pays off all week.

## Discussion (protect this time)

- Which task in your department would you hand to an agent today, and which one never? What separates them?
- Where exactly does useful become risky in your workflow: which single step carries the blast radius?
- Who is your Gary at your real company, and what happens the Friday they leave?

## Decisions the council owes the company

- Which single workflow gets your first agent (your Agent Role Definition candidate).
- The first boundary: one thing your agent must never do, written down before you build anything.

When the session is complete and the debrief has run, the group advances with /parade-advance.
