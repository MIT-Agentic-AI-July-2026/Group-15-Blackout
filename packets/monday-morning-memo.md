# AirMail: The Monday Morning Memo

| | |
| - | - |
| From | Gale Womplerton III, CEO |
| To | The AI Transformation Council |
| Cc | Marcus Ledgerwood, CFO |
| Sent | Monday, 07:00 |
| Subject | The wall, in numbers |

Welcome aboard. That was the greeting. It is over.

You read the packet. You know who we are. This memo is what we are up against. I will give it to you in numbers, because numbers are the only language this week respects.

Read it on your phone if you must. Read the table at a desk.

**Thursday morning: the parade.**

The Grand Harvest Parade flies Thursday morning. 40 million people watch it on television.

The contract is worth $6.5M a year. It calls for 16 giant balloons and 380,000 cubic feet of helium in parade month. Liquidated damages if we fail to deliver: $2M.

The reputational damages are not in the contract. I promise you they are real.

This company has never missed a parade. We are not starting Thursday.

**Thursday evening: the board.**

Hours after the last balloon is walked back into the hangar, I stand in front of my board.

Revenue has been $84M for three straight years. Flat is not a plateau. Flat is a leak with good manners.

Sharp Objects Capital owns 8.2 percent of us, and Nadia Pinn writes letters. The letters are cordial. The arithmetic inside them is not. She is not wrong about our speed, which is what makes the letters hard to file.

Thursday evening I need to show that board a company that moves.

**Nine days: AutoPlex.**

The AutoPlex renewal decision lands in nine days.

$23.3M a year. 2,400 dealerships. Our single largest revenue line.

The contract runs on the Wacky Waving Uptime SLA: 99.5 percent fleet uptime, $50K per day in penalties during promotional periods. Five consecutive breach days give them the right to walk.

Dale has kept this account healthy on airline miles and apologies. He needs better tools than apologies.

Nine days. Count with me.

**Friday: Gary.**

Gary Baszo retires Friday. 43 years.

He is one of four Level IV Master Riggers in this company. Level IV is not a courtesy title. It is a legal requirement for flying the big balloons. Friday we go from four to three.

Certification takes 18 months. The math does not care how we feel about it.

What Gary knows now lives in the Gary Graph. After Friday, the graph is what we have.

Ask it things this week, while the man himself is still down the hall to tell you when it is wrong.

**The contingency table.**

That was the wall. Here is the part that keeps me up at night.

A while ago I asked Marcus one question: what happens to this company if demand ever truly finds our hero SKU. Not campaign demand. Internet demand. The kind that does not warn you first.

His answer is below, unedited. Read the table twice.

From: Marcus Ledgerwood, CFO

Per Gale's question, I modeled a hypothetical demand event against the Colossal Swan ($89, 55 percent margin, our best-loved product). I assumed a spike of 40 times baseline. For the record, 40x events are rare. Also for the record, I have watched three of them happen to other companies, from the safety of a spreadsheet. The spreadsheet was not safe either.

The manual math, using every person we employ:

| Line | Capacity today | Modeled event | The gap |
| - | - | - | - |
| Support | 262 tickets per day (six people, 11 minutes per ticket) | 16,000 tickets per day | 61 times our capacity |
| Swan production | 1,200 per week on two shifts; 4,100 with a third | 48,000 per week | nearly 12 times our ceiling with every shift lit |
| Contract penalties | $0 | $2.3M modeled exposure | roughly two thirds of a typical year's net income |

I modeled overtime. Overtime does not close a 61x gap. I modeled emergency hiring. Hiring does not close it inside a quarter, and an event of this kind does not schedule an intake call first.

I would ordinarily attach three scenarios and recommend none of them. This time there is one scenario, and I cannot recommend it either. I can only report that it does not require our permission to happen.

Respectfully submitted,
Marcus

**Why you exist.**

That table is the reason there is a council.

If a wave like that ever hits us, no manual version of this company survives the week it arrives in. I cannot hire across a 61x gap. Nobody can. Nobody ever will.

What I can do is field the six teams reading this memo: people who can put machines under the load and keep humans on the judgment.

Decide what the agents carry. Decide what we carry. Be ready to defend the line between the two, to my board, on Thursday, with numbers.

Build like the wave is coming. Waves do not check calendars.

My door is open. My patience is Thursday-shaped.

Gale
