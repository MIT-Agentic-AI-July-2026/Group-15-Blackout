# Grand Inflation Industries: Welcome Packet

*Parade Week edition. For the incoming AI Transformation Council. Read before Act I. Bring it with you: the back half is reference.*

## A Letter From the CEO

You said yes. Good.

Here is what you joined. Grand Inflation Industries is 77 years old. My grandfather Gusty came home from the war with three skills: rigging, vulcanizing rubber, and overpromising. He aimed all three at joy. First product: a weather balloon dressed up as a car lot attraction. He never looked back. Neither did we.

Today we make parade balloons, stadium mascots, tube men, pool floats, and bounce houses. We make joy that holds air. I believe in this company completely. I am also the only person here allowed to say that it might die.

Revenue is $84 million. It was $84 million last year. It was $84 million the year before that. People call that stable. It is not stable. It is a slow leak with good manners.

The board meets Thursday. The Grand Harvest Parade flies Thursday morning, on national television, the way it has since before I was born. Gary Baszo retires Friday after 43 years, and half of what this company knows walks out with him unless we hold onto it on purpose. An activist fund owns 8.2 percent of us and writes very polite letters. My COO chair has been empty for eight months.

I did not fill that chair. I hired you instead.

You are the AI Transformation Council. You are my bet: that this company can learn to move at the speed the world now moves without losing what it is. Six departments. The full program. Every system we own, opened to you.

I do not need a deck. I need the swans to ship.

Read this packet tonight. Learn the company. Monday at 07:00 I will send you the numbers.

Gale Womplerton III
Chief Executive Officer

## The Shape of the Week

The full program. One company. One shared objective: **end Thursday with the Company Health Index at 70 or better and a board-credible plan.**

The Company Health Index is four dials, 0 to 100, live all week in your Field Guide, in the panel called "Your company, right now":

| Dial | Measures | Monday reading |
| - | - | - |
| Cash | liquidity and margin discipline | 62 |
| Customer Trust | sentiment, satisfaction, subscriber health | 58 |
| Operational Reliability | fulfillment, uptime, production integrity | 55 |
| Compliance and Control | safety, privacy, governance posture | 70 |

The win condition is shared: the average of the four dials at 70 or better at the final board meeting. Departments are one company, not rivals. Nobody wins in a company that loses.

- **Act I: learn the company.** Walk its systems, map its work, and staff your department's first digital teammate. By the end of the act, you have an agent doing real work against real data.
- **Act II: put it under load.** Single agents have limits. You will find them, then build past them: agents that coordinate with each other, and with you.
- **Act III: take command.** Governance, control, and judgment: what your agents may do alone, what needs your signature, and what stays human forever. Then the board, with the numbers you made.

## Ground Rules

1. **The numbers are real and they reconcile.** Unit economics, SLA math, the helium ledger: it all adds up, on purpose. Check it. Arithmetic is a fair weapon here, same as at home.
2. **Everything your agents do is logged, and that is a feature.** Every tool call lands in company telemetry. The log is how you will know what your agents actually did, as opposed to what you meant them to do. Operators read logs. You are operators now.
3. **Play it straight.** The world is absurd. The people in it are not. Inside these walls, tube man uptime is a matter of total professional seriousness, and the week works best when you treat it that way.
4. **Have fun.** The company makes joy that holds air. It deserves a council that enjoys saving it.

Welcome to Toledo. See you Monday.
